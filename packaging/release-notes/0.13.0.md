StrataOS 0.13.0: single sign-on for your apps, the system disk where you want it (its own disk, a mirror, or the data disks), Debian upgrades proposed by the NAS itself, smart photos, files on demand in the desktop app, hardware video transcoding, power schedules, routines that act, and task logs in your language.

## Installation

- **Already on 0.12.x**: **Updates → Update StrataOS** in the web interface.
- New installs: `amd64` / `arm64` ISO (now with three entries: own disk, system on two disks, system on the data disks), `strataos-0.13.0-raspberrypi.img.xz`, or `sudo apt install ./strataos_0.13.0_<arch>.deb`. The desktop app has its own release with files on demand.

## New

**Sign-in and system**
- **Single sign-on for apps**: the NAS is the OpenID Connect provider of its apps (Grafana and Paperless-ngx set up by the App Center; Nextcloud, Immich, Gitea, Mealie and any other app with the settings to copy), with access rules by user and group, consent, two-factor when you ask for it, and sign-out through the NAS. App domains can also ask for a NAS sign-in before the app (forward authentication): the app receives who you are, never a header someone made up.
- **System disk, your way**: StrataOS lives on its own disk, mirrored on two disks, or on the data disks themselves (a copy of the system on each, RAID 1, and a data partition with the rest of each disk for your volumes). Choose at installation or in the setup wizard, and move between these later from Storage → Disks → System disk; every step that erases a disk lists the disks and asks you to type their names. A mirrored system starts from any of its disks; system backups and restores from the installer.
- **System upgrade (Debian)**: the NAS finds the next major Debian release in the Debian archive itself, waits for its first point release, checks itself every day (space, packages, kernel and its modules, boot loader, and a simulation of the upgrade: StrataOS must stay installable) and then asks "Do you want to upgrade the system to Debian N?". Now or in the maintenance window: a restore point first, the upgrade as a task with its log, the restart and the checks after it, and back to the restore point on its own if anything fails. Failed checks are listed with how to fix them; an administrator can still force the upgrade after a warning.

**Files, photos and media**
- **Files on demand** in the desktop app (Linux, Windows; macOS with a signed build): every file of the NAS shows at once and is downloaded when opened.
- **Smart photos**: people (faces grouped on the NAS, never sent elsewhere), things, places on a map and memories, search by description. Documents are found by what they contain with the full-text search of 0.12.1.
- **Hardware transcoding**: the graphics card (Intel, AMD, NVIDIA) converts videos for the built-in player, with the reason shown when the processor has to take over, and is given to Jellyfin or Plex in one click.

**Network, power and virtual machines**
- **Power**: scheduled shutdowns, restarts and power-ons (by the clock of the motherboard), daylight saving time handled, postponed while a task runs or someone uses the NAS; Wake-on-LAN that stays on after a restart, and a Wake up button in the multi-NAS console.
- **Virtual networks** shared between NAS (VXLAN, optionally encrypted with WireGuard); incremental image backups of running VMs; S3 bucket versioning and object lock.
- **macOS VMs**: one button removes the recovery images no macOS VM uses anymore; the creation form warns when the processor has no invariant TSC (as when StrataOS itself runs in a VM).

**Monitoring and automation**
- **Routines can act**: take a snapshot, start a backup, start or stop a VM or a container, update an app, as tasks named after the routine.
- **Storage trends**: a file hard-linked into several shared folders counts once.
- **Task logs in every language** of the interface (command output stays as it is).
- **Configuration backup**: the firewall, virtual networks, sign-in providers, two-factor policy, S3 service and keys, backup repositories, smart photos, single sign-on and desktop preferences are exported and imported too.

## Under the hood

- The end-to-end tests on a virtual NAS (about 2,100 checks) run on GitHub: a smoke subset on every pull request, the whole suite every night and on each release tag. Android lint runs on every change of the app.
- API tokens cannot approve the sign-in of an app (single sign-on).

## Known limits

- The automatic way back of the system upgrade needs restore points by snapshot (systems installed from the StrataOS ISO, or on LVM); other systems are told before they start.
- Moving the system between a mirror and the data disks takes two moves, through a disk of its own.
